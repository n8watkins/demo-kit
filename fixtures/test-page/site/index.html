The Quiet Work of Lighthouses

6 min read

Long before satellites, a coastline was read in light. Each tower kept its own rhythm, a pattern of flashes that told a passing crew exactly which headland they were looking at.

A keeper's day was mostly patience: trimming wicks, polishing lenses, and writing the weather into a log that nobody might read for years.

The lamps are automatic now, but the habit they taught is worth keeping. Do the small thing well, at the same hour, and let it add up to something a stranger can steer by.

Some towers still open their doors on summer weekends. Climb the stairs once and the view explains the job better than any history.
